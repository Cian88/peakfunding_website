A French bank does not decide on an application form: it decides on a file that a credit analyst has to understand quickly. Who is borrowing, for which project, how much, with what resources and what risks. A clear, consistent and documented application does not guarantee approval, but it avoids back-and-forth and lets the bank rule on substance rather than on missing documents.

What the credit analyst looks for first

  • The request: the loan amount, term and purpose, stated without ambiguity.
  • The borrowers: family situation, employment status, seniority, income and outgoings of each person.
  • The project: price, works, notary and guarantee fees, use of the property (primary residence, buy-to-let, second home).
  • The financing plan: down payment, requested loan and other resources. Total resources must equal the total cost of the project.
  • Repayment capacity: the share of income spent on loans and what is left to live on.

The HCSF rules: 35% debt ratio and 25 years

Since 1 January 2022, the decision of France's High Council for Financial Stability (HCSF) has been binding on banks. The debt-to-income ratio must not exceed 35% of net income, borrower insurance included. The loan term is capped at 25 years, extended to 27 years when a deferred repayment period comes with the purchase of a new home or of an older home with major works.

Banks may depart from these criteria for 20% of their new mortgage lending each quarter, mainly for primary-residence purchases. An application outside the criteria is therefore not impossible, but it must be particularly strong and well presented.

Disposable income and stable earnings

The debt ratio is not enough. Each bank also assesses the disposable income left once loans are paid, using its own criteria. It looks at how stable income is: a permanent contract past the probation period, civil-service status, time in the job. For self-employed people and company directors, it works from the average of recent financial years. Expected rent from a buy-to-let is usually only partly taken into account, often at 70%.

Down payment and remaining savings

Most banks expect the down payment to cover at least the fees: notary fees of around 7 to 8% of the price for an older property and 2 to 3% for a new one, plus guarantee and arrangement fees. The savings left after the purchase reassure as much as the down payment itself. Their origin must be documented: personal savings, sale of a property, a gift or a family loan.

Accounts: what your statements reveal

The statements of all your accounts for the last three months are read closely. Repeated overdrafts, payment incidents, a consumer loan taken out just before the application or spending inconsistent with the stated budget weaken a file. Declared figures must be visible in the statements: salary, rent received, current loan repayments.

SCI, company, self-employed: what changes

  • Through an SCI (French property company): the bank analyses the company and each of its partners. It asks for the articles of association, a recent Kbis extract, the general-meeting minutes authorising the loan and, if the SCI already exists, its latest accounts. Partners often stand as guarantors.
  • Through an operating company or a holding: the company's balance sheets, income statement and debt matter as much as the directors' income.
  • Self-employed and liberal professions: the last two or three sets of accounts, tax returns and tax notices establish the income the bank will retain.

Presenting the application: a note, a checklist, figures that reconcile

A well-presented application starts with a summary note of a few pages: the project, the borrowers, the financing plan and the points that need explaining, before anyone raises them. Every figure must be traceable to a supporting document. The budget must equal the down payment plus the loan; agency fees must not be counted twice if they are already included in the price. A document checklist per person and per company avoids forgetting anything when the file is sent.

Mistakes that cost weeks

  • Missing, illegible or out-of-date documents (statements older than three months, an expired Kbis extract).
  • Amounts that do not match between the form, the payslips and the statements.
  • A financing plan that does not balance, or a down payment whose origin is not documented.
  • A consumer loan taken out while the project is being prepared.
  • Applications sent to several banks with different figures.

Preparing alone or with support

Nothing requires you to use an intermediary: you can prepare and present your application yourself, to your own bank or to several lenders. A broker builds the file, presents it to partner banks and negotiates the terms; fees are normally due only if the financing succeeds. Either way, the quality of the presentation is the first thing a bank sees.
